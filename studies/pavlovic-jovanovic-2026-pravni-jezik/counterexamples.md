# Counterexamples and anti-rules

1. **Nominalization is not automatically bureaucracy.**  
   In a statute or contract, `po podnošenju zahteva` may be compact and conventional.

2. **Passive voice is not automatically bad Serbian.**  
   `Zahtev se podnosi u roku od osam dana.` can appropriately foreground procedure rather than actor.

3. **Repetition can protect legal identity.**  
   Replacing every repeated defined term with a synonym may introduce ambiguity.

4. **Long sentences are not automatically errors.**  
   A sentence can legitimately encode conditions and exceptions; the issue is whether dependencies remain interpretable.

5. **Shorter is not always clearer.**  
   Removing a repeated condition can make the legal scope less explicit.

6. **Indeterminacy is not always accidental vagueness.**  
   A general clause may intentionally cover a class of future cases.

7. **Legal formality is not an AI-style signal.**  
   Specialized terminology, cross-references and impersonal forms can be genre requirements.

8. **Legal register does not excuse everything.**  
   Unnecessary accumulation, broken syntax and redundant layers can still be editing problems; register is a boundary, not immunity.

9. **Historical legal forms are not modern targets.**  
   Archaic examples in the article must never seed current spelling/grammar behavior.

10. **Political and legal bureaucratese are not interchangeable.**  
    Similar surface features can serve different institutional functions.
