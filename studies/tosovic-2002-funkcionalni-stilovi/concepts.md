# Reusable source model

## 1. Style is selection plus organization

The source repeatedly treats style as a way of selecting and combining/organizing linguistic resources for a particular communicative domain. A form is therefore not stylistically interpretable in isolation.

**Project consequence:** the humanizer must judge constructions relative to function, genre and surrounding organization, not a universal style blacklist.

## 2. Functional style is extra-linguistically conditioned

Functional styles arise from human activity, communicative purpose, participants, channel, medium and social setting, then acquire recurring linguistic organization.

**Project consequence:** infer task, audience, channel and genre before making stylistic edits.

## 3. Language norm and stylistic norm are not the same

The source explicitly distinguishes standard-language norm from stylistic norm and treats their degree/character of obligatoriness as different.

**Project consequence:** only `NORM` can hard-block. Register preference belongs to `EDITING` / `NATIVE_USAGE`, with contextual strength rather than universal obligation.

## 4. Functional styles form a hierarchy, not five isolated boxes

The proposed system includes style complexes, five major functional styles, interstyles, substyles and genres. Boundaries can be porous and switching/mixing can be productive.

**Project consequence:** support mixed profiles and section-level register shifts. Do not force an entire document into one flat style.

## 5. Receiver and channel matter

The source models communication via sender, receiver, channel, code, prior information and noise. The same content can require different packaging depending on who must decode it and through which medium.

**Project consequence:** readability cannot be reduced to sentence length or vocabulary difficulty; account for audience expertise and channel.

## 6. Economy and redundancy are competing functional tendencies

The conclusion frames functional styles partly as a balance between economy (shorter transmission) and redundancy (more reliable transmission).

**Project consequence:** neither brevity nor repetition is universally better. Ask what information loss, ambiguity or decoding cost each edit creates.

## 7. Standardization and clichés can be functional

Administrative style, and to a degree publicistic style, use standardization, unification and recurring formulas to reduce decoding time and improve reliability. The book explicitly warns against treating such mechanisms as stylistic ballast by default.

**Project consequence:** template regularity can be legitimate genre behavior and must not automatically become an AI-style finding.

## 8. Publicistic style combines information and influence

Publicistic writing targets a broad audience, values accessibility and can combine referential/informational work with persuasion, emotional impact and rhetorical devices. It is highly genre-diverse and porous.

**Project consequence:** public writing may legitimately be clearer, more concrete, rhythmic or emotionally marked than scientific prose; persuasion itself is not a defect.

## 9. Scientific style prioritizes objectivity and conceptual structure

The source characterizes scientific expression through logic, abstraction, objectivity, precision, terminology, restricted lexical selection and often complex multi-clause syntax.

**Project consequence:** complexity, technical vocabulary and impersonal structure can be functional; “humanizing” science into casual prose can destroy precision.

## 10. Administrative style prioritizes standardization/unification

Documents are shaped by recurring structures, formulaic elements and genre expectations; internal textual standardization is described as constitutive for this style.

**Project consequence:** preserve standardized formulas and repeated fields when they serve institutional processing or referential stability.

## 11. Conversational style is context-rich and structurally economical

Conversational style relies heavily on situation, intonation and non-verbal information, and commonly uses simple/elliptical syntax, pauses, repetitions, fragments and frozen formulas.

**Project consequence:** fragments, ellipsis, particles and repetitions should often be preserved in dialogue/chat instead of “repairing” them into written exposition.

## 12. Literary-artistic style permits markedness and de-automation

The literary style has the widest expressive freedom and uses structural foregrounding, rhythm, figures and deviations for aesthetic function.

**Project consequence:** unusual syntax, repetition, metaphor or norm-adjacent markedness in creative writing requires especially strong restraint.

## 13. A style profile is partly informational

The source distinguishes semantic content from formal/stylistic information about sender, receiver, process and code.

**Project consequence:** edits can preserve proposition while deleting style-bearing information. Semantic preservation alone is insufficient.
