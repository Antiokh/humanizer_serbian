# Counterexamples and anti-rules

1. **Direct disagreement is not automatically rude or non-native.**  
   Project example: `Ne slažem se. Mislim da ovde gubimo najvažniji deo.` In a neutral work discussion this can be fully natural.

2. **Indirectness is not automatically better Serbian.**  
   A stack of `možda / čini mi se / možda bismo mogli` can be appropriate, but it can also reflect English interactional packaging or excessive caution.

3. **Discourse markers are not filler by default.**  
   `Pa, mislim da nije baš tako.` can use `pa` and `mislim` to manage stance. Deleting both may alter force.

4. **Conciseness is not automatically interactional loss.**  
   `Ne mogu danas.` may be perfectly appropriate when relationship and context make explanation unnecessary.

5. **A subtitle becoming more direct does not prove a Serbian preference.**  
   Character limits and timing can force compression.

6. **A marker surviving translation does not make it “English-like”.**  
   Serbian has its own ordinary particles, hedges, vocatives and stance frames.

7. **`ti` is not inherently informal-error and `vi` is not inherently better.**  
   Relationship, institution, age, role and local interaction determine the choice.

8. **Film dialogue is not a population corpus.**  
   A ratio found in ten films cannot become a general statement about all Serbian speakers.

9. **Strong agreement is not a humanizer target.**  
   The source's corpus tendency does not justify rewriting every `da, ali` into categorical agreement.

10. **Text alone may underdetermine force.**  
    Irony, challenge and reassurance can depend on intonation and non-verbal behavior outside the humanizer's input.
